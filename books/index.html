Book 1

Behold Your Mother

Every night his mother holds out her arm in the dark and asks him to tell her that God is Love. Chris knows how many times is enough.

The Point, 1993. Chris is eight and his mother’s only company. By day Evelyn counsels other people’s children for the Board of Education. At night the laundry lies wet on both beds, and her son sleeps underneath it, behind a door she locks with a key on a shoelace. Chris counts everything, including the lies he tells to keep her safe.

He copies a stranger’s novena to St. Jude forty times, asking to be thin, and slides every copy under a locked church door. That fall his grandfather begins to waste away, and Chris starts to wonder where his prayers are going.

His grandmother Anne is allowed in on Saturdays, as far as his shelf. By her phone is the number of the child welfare bureau, with one straight line through it. “If I call it, your mother shuts the door,” she tells him. “Then I don’t have Saturdays either.”

So she fights for Wednesdays. One night a week Chris sleeps in dry sheets, six floors above the water. On Thursdays he goes back to Rachel Street.

The first novel of the Aspartame series, followed by Nobody Sent Me.

Book 2 · 251 pages · November 2026

Nobody Sent Me

Did you eat your lunch.
Did you say your prayers.
Did you wear an undershirt.
Did anybody ask you anything.
Is it all right?

The Point, 1998. Chris is thirteen, and every weekday at twenty to seven his mother rings twice, showers at his grandmother’s, and asks him the same four questions. Then one more, looking at the window. Someone always tells her that it is.

He is thinner by a summer of walking, and on his way up the hill to a school his grandmother is paying for with a failing heart. His mother counsels other people’s children for the Board of Education. On Saturdays she stands outside the church fence at Star of the Sea, at the one bar worn down to bright iron, and prays through the five o’clock Mass without going in.

By the summer he finishes school, his mother sleeps sitting up on a bench under the highway ramp and will not come in from the street. A college across the country wants to know when he will arrive.

One night he sits on the next bench until dawn. When she wakes, she asks who sent him.

“Nobody sent me. I came.”

The second novel of the Aspartame series, after Behold Your Mother. It stands on its own.
