Cliffword Privacy Policy

Effective date: 20 November 2026

Cliffword is a language-learning app for iPhone and Android, made by Scott Chanatry, an individual developer ("I" and "me" below). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the app.

The short version

  • Cliffword has no accounts, no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no crash reporting.
  • Everything you do in the app stays on your phone: your progress, your settings, the lyrics and texts you paste, the songs you add, and the photo of you, if you choose one for your picture.
  • The language model and speech recognition run on your phone.
  • I do not run a server that receives anything from the app. I do not collect, sell or share your personal information.

What the app stores on your phone

Cliffword keeps these in its own private storage on your phone (a database only the app can open):

  • your learning progress: the words you know, your answers, the stories you have heard, the sessions you can resume, and your minutes spoken;
  • your settings, such as voice, slang regions, text display and the explicit lyrics switch, and which character is your picture in the story;
  • a photo of you, only if you take a selfie or choose a photo for your picture (see "Your picture in the story");
  • songs you add: title, artist, and for Apple Music songs the identifier and an artwork link;
  • lyrics you paste and texts you bring (articles, transcripts);
  • songs you share to Cliffword from another app (usually a link and a song name);
  • the text of what you said, as part of your progress and saved sessions;
  • the language model file, once you download it.

None of this is sent to me or to anyone else by the app.

Microphone and speech recognition

  • Cliffword uses the microphone only while you hold the screen to speak or sing. It asks for permission the first time.
  • Your speech is turned into text by your phone's own on-device speech recognizer. Cliffword does not save recordings of your voice.
  • Network recognition is a setting, off by default. If you turn it on, and your phone cannot recognize Spanish on the device, your phone's speech service sends your voice to Apple (on iPhone) or Google (on Android) to be recognized. They handle it under their own privacy policies. I never receive it.
  • On Android 12 and older, phones cannot be told to recognize speech only on the device. There, unless you turn on network recognition, you type your answers instead.
  • If you do not allow the microphone or speech recognition, you can type your answers instead.

Your picture in the story

Your answers in a story show a picture of you. You can pick one of the drawn characters, or use a photo of yourself. The photo is optional.

  • Cliffword asks for the camera only when you tap "Take a selfie". When you tap "Choose a photo", your phone's own photo picker opens and Cliffword receives only the photo you pick, not the rest of your library.
  • Cliffword keeps one copy of that photo in its private storage on your phone and uses it only as your picture in the app. It is never uploaded, never sent to me or anyone else, and never used for anything else. It is left out of your phone's backups.
  • You can delete it at any time: Settings, Your picture, Remove photo (choosing a character or another photo deletes it too). Deleting the app deletes it.

The language model download

The app needs a language model file (about 1.1 GB). It asks before downloading it. The file comes from Hugging Face (huggingface.co and its content delivery network) or from a copy hosted for Cliffword. As with any download, the server that sends the file sees your IP address and the basic details your phone includes in a web request. Cliffword sends no account, no identifier and none of your learning data with that request. Hugging Face's privacy policy covers its servers. Once downloaded, the model runs on your phone. Your answers are never sent to an online model.

Apple Music (iPhone)

If you connect Apple Music, Cliffword uses Apple's MusicKit to list the songs in your library and play them. Those requests go between your phone and Apple under your Apple Account and Apple's privacy policy, and album artwork loads from Apple's servers. Cliffword keeps the songs you open in its database on your phone. I receive nothing from Apple Music. You can turn off Cliffword's access to Apple Music in your phone's Settings at any time.

Spotify and other apps

Cliffword does not connect to Spotify. When you share a song from the Spotify app (or any app) to Cliffword, Cliffword receives what that app's share sheet sends, usually a link and the song's name, and keeps it on your phone. If you open a link from Cliffword, it opens in the app or website it belongs to, and that service's own privacy policy applies.

Voices

Cliffword reads stories aloud with your phone's built-in voices. This happens on your phone.

Backups

Your phone's own backups (iCloud Backup on iPhone, Google's device backup on Android) may include Cliffword's data, such as your progress, lyrics and texts. These backups are made by your phone, go to your own Apple or Google account, and are under your control and their terms. The language model file and the photo for your picture are left out of backups. I cannot see your backups.

What Apple and Google may share with me

Apple and Google may show me download numbers and other statistics without names. If you choose, in your phone's settings, to share crash reports or diagnostics with app developers, they may give me crash reports for Cliffword (for example, the phone model, the system version and where the app stopped). These come from Apple and Google under their terms. They never include your lyrics, texts or progress, because the app does not send them.

If you contact me

If you email me, I receive your email address and whatever you write. I use them only to answer you, and I delete the conversation when it is no longer needed.

Children

Cliffword is not directed to children under 13. By default it shows explicit slang and lyrics. The app does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Your choices and deleting your data

Because I hold no data about you, there is nothing for me to look up, correct, export or delete. Everything is on your phone, and you control it:

  • Delete lyrics and texts: Settings, Delete all lyrics and texts. Your songs stay in the list, without lyrics.
  • Reset progress: Settings, Reset progress. This clears the words you know, your stories, sessions and minutes spoken.
  • Delete the language model: Settings, Delete the model.
  • Delete your photo: Settings, Your picture, Remove photo.
  • Delete everything: uninstall Cliffword. This removes all the data the app stored on your phone (copies in your own phone backups are kept until those backups are replaced or deleted).
  • Permissions: turn off the microphone, speech recognition, camera or Apple Music access for Cliffword in your phone's Settings.

Selling and sharing

I do not sell or share your personal information, and I do not use it for advertising. The app does not track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Security

Cliffword's data sits in the app's private storage, which your phone's operating system keeps separate from other apps and protects with your phone's passcode and encryption.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted at this address with a new effective date. If Cliffword ever starts collecting any information, this policy and the app's store privacy labels will say so before the version that does it is released.
