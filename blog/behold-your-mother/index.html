Behold Your Mother

November 11, 2026

My first novel is out.

Behold Your Mother begins on a first floor balcony, with an eight year old boy caramelizing his mother’s sweetener in a bottle cap. He sets the amber beads on the ledge in a row, the way a rosary begins. It smells like his grandmother’s kitchen on a Saturday.

The boy is Chris. He lives on the Point, a waterfront neighborhood in a city I made up, with his mother, Evelyn. By day she counsels other people’s children for the Board of Education. At night she holds out her arm in the dark and asks him to tell her that God is Love, and Chris knows how many times is enough. His grandmother Anne lives six floors above the water. She is allowed in on Saturdays, as far as his shelf, and she fights for one night a week.

I have spent most of my working life as a child psychiatrist, listening to children and to the families around them until the picture makes sense. This book is not a case. It is a story about what a child sees and counts and keeps to himself, and about the adults who love him in the ways they can.

The blue packet on the cover is the sweetener from that first page. It is also the mark of the series. Behold Your Mother is the first book of the Aspartame novels, and each book will follow Chris a little further. The second, Nobody Sent Me, takes him from thirteen to the summer he finishes school, and it is coming soon.

A few things I hope you’ll know going in. The book is quiet. It moves at the speed of a boy’s attention. It doesn’t explain itself, and some of what happens is never explained at all, because children aren’t told. If you’ve ever been the one in a family who kept watch, I think you’ll recognize him.
