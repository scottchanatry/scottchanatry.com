Chapter One: Aspartame

November 13, 2026

The opening of Behold Your Mother, the first Aspartame novel.

Aspartame caramelized surprisingly well. Summer sweetness filled the air.

In the nook of a first floor balcony overlooking a wildly quiet block, his side filled with a continuous progression of identical three story apartments, the smoke simmering into the apartment adjacent on the right for there was only one orientation obvious to him. On the other side sat a mixture of houses he regarded with care.

Inside Chris’s mother ate canned tuna and was not aware of the nearby blaze engulfing a few of her most prized possessions. She usually decorated her tuna with Sweet N Low. Chris was accomplice to an attempt today to burrow Equal from the coffee counter of the local Deli and kept a few packets for himself.

Lighters were not hard to find in an apartment ordained by more candles to the Saints than a small Church.

There were fourteen of them going at all hours, the tall glass kind that burned for a week, with a Saint on the front and a prayer on the back. His mother counted them every night by sevens. She always knew when one was about to go out. There were Bic lighters in the silverware drawer and on both windowsills and in the pockets of her housecoat, and one in the soap dish where it had no business being. The lighter in Chris’s hand was green. Twenty minutes ago it had been St. Jude’s. Jude was the patron of hopeless causes and Chris figured he would understand.

He started out by burning one packet whole, right on the concrete. The paper went brown and then black and curled up and blew off. The powder inside melted and bubbled and went the color of the top of Grandma’s crumb cake. It smelled so much like her kitchen on a Sunday that he had to sit down.

After that he did it more carefully. He had a bottle cap he had been carrying in his pocket since May. He tore each packet open along the edge and poured it into the cap and held the lighter underneath until the powder turned to liquid. When it cooled he could pop it out with his thumbnail, a hard little dome the color of the St. Michael window at Star of the Sea. He had three of them on the ledge. A rosary starts with three beads like that, for faith and hope and charity, and he had thought of that when he lined them up. He had one packet left, down in his sock.

On the back of every packet in very small letters it said PHENYLKETONURICS: CONTAINS PHENYLALANINE. Chris had sounded it out under his breath on the walk home. He decided the Phenylketonurics must be an order, like the Franciscans, men in brown robes somewhere upstate who had taken a vow against it. He felt bad for them. He was also a little jealous. At least somebody had told them in writing what they couldn’t have.
